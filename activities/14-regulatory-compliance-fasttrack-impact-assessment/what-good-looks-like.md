**Expected output structure**
A strong AI output for this activity will contain the following sections / elements:
- Three independent DPDP impact matrices (one per analyst)
- Consensus scorecard: which obligations all three flagged non-compliant
- Red Team: specifically-named obligations the three analysts missed (children's data, cross-border notified countries, SDF classification)
- Final investment plan: top 3 priorities with INR estimate + timeline + owner + penalty exposure
- Phased compliance roadmap aligned with DPDP Phase 1/2/3 deadlines
**Scoring rubric (5 dimensions)**
**Common failure modes — watch for these**
- Analyst gives generic 'non-compliant' without citing SOP. Response: 'quote the specific SOP line that creates the gap'.
- Cost estimates are H/M/L without reasoning. Response: 'what SPECIFIC change requires how much? Build the cost from the components.'
- Red Team misses children's data. Response: 'look at DP007 in the inventory. What does DPDP require for children's data?'
**Debrief question for the room**
Where did the AI add the most value — and where would you NOT trust it without a human review step? Be specific about the failure mode you saw.
Scoring is a facilitation device — not an exam. The point is to make the quality dimensions visible and discussable, so participants internalize what a 'good' AI output looks like for their own work.
