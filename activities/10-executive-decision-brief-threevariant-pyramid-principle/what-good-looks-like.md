**Expected output structure**
A strong AI output for this activity will contain the following sections / elements:
- Three variant decks (tactical, strategic, risk-focused), each 5 slides
- Mistral synthesized winning deck integrating strongest elements of each
- Two-minute acceptance test result (facilitator reads aloud)
- Executive deck ready for Monday 09:00 WOR
**Scoring rubric (5 dimensions)**
**Common failure modes — watch for these**
- Three variants are three rewrites. Response: 'the TACTICAL variant must open with 'what happened, what we did'. STRATEGIC with 'what this means for Q4'. RISK with 'what could go wrong'.'
- Deck leads with background. Response: 'cut slides 1 and 2 — start with the answer'.
- Final ask is vague. Response: 'what SPECIFICALLY are you asking the CEO to approve? One sentence.'
**Debrief question for the room**
Where did the AI add the most value — and where would you NOT trust it without a human review step? Be specific about the failure mode you saw.
Scoring is a facilitation device — not an exam. The point is to make the quality dimensions visible and discussable, so participants internalize what a 'good' AI output looks like for their own work.
